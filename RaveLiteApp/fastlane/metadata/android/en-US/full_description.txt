<b>For Ravers and Super Heroes.</b> Fit for the floor, all night. Ready
for intensive dancing. Able to defend yourself. Of balanced and
positively energised spirit.

A five-element altar that lives in your pocket. It chimes through the
day, and each call asks for one small thing — ten push-ups, a minute of
breath, a glass of water, a kick you are learning. Answer it in three
seconds from the notification and get on with your life. By evening you
have trained, without ever having <i>gone</i> to train.

RaveLite is not a fitness app, a habit tracker or a notification
scheduler. Those are surface mechanics. It is a ritual companion for the
raver as urban shaman — reclaiming a desk-broken body for ecstatic
practice.

<b>The five elements</b>

Air is breath and the open chest. Earth is the core and the rooted
pelvis. Water is flow, fascia and hydration — no pool required. Fire is
capacity and output. Heart conducts. The work is named in element
language so the body knows what it is doing before the mind reads the
screen.

<b>What it does</b>

• <b>Chimes, not nags.</b> A call every half hour or so is a temporal
  anchor, closer to a temple bell than a Slack ping. Each element has its
  own sound and its own vibration, so the body learns the call without
  looking.
• <b>Daily Sets.</b> Seventeen tracks — push, pull, row, squat, core,
  hangs, posture, breath, mobility, stillness, kicks, flow — each asking
  for sets at about half your tested max, spread across the day. They
  climb when you keep up and ease off when you don't.
• <b>A character sheet.</b> Fifteen attributes that grow from what you
  actually did. Every element at par in one day is a Harmony day.
• <b>Curricula worth learning.</b> Dance, martial basics, staff and flow,
  yoga and tai chi, jumps, running, military test standards — switch on
  what you came for.
• <b>It explains itself.</b> Every drill, track and attribute has a card
  saying what it is, how to do it, and why it is there.

<b>It fits the life you have</b>

Ten archetypes, from The Raver through The Guardian, The Monk and The
Flow Artist to The Night Shift and The Parent who gets ten minutes at a
time. Say how much day you have and everything sizes to fit — nine
chimes or two, the same proportion of you either way. A shorter day is a
full day's training, not a failed one.

Travelling, hurt, at a festival, or taking the day off? Say so, and the
app bends round the week instead of you working round the app. Each one
expires by itself.

<b>Your data</b>

No account, no server, no analytics, no ads. Everything you log lives on
your phone and is sent nowhere. The only network request is to
Open-Meteo for the forecast, carrying a location rounded to about 10 km.
Settings has an export that writes the whole thing to a file you choose.

<b>Beta</b>

One person's app, opened up. It is not a medical device and gives no
medical advice. Stop if it hurts, and talk to a doctor first if you have
reason to.
