# Practice: paths, tiers and charts

*21 Sep 2026.* The operator asked for far more variety per flow prop,
for dance, and for dance combat — "it's hard to invent or research them
on my own, and I bet that goes for everyone" — with difficulty like DDR:
Basic, Intermediate and Advanced, and within them Beginner, Light,
Standard, Heavy and Challenge.

## The model

DDR has songs, and five charts for each song. Here a **move** is the
song and the **chart** is how hard you play it.

- Every move on a path has a **tier**: basic, intermediate or advanced —
  how far into the discipline it sits.
- Every move can be played at every **chart**. Standard is the move's own
  dose. The other four bend it the same way for every move in a family,
  so no move needs five doses written for it:

| Chart | Flow props | Dance | Dance combat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner | 1 min each side, half tempo, reset after every drop | count it out, no music, 8 clean reps | slow motion, no music, 5 each side |
| Light | 2 min, both directions, easy tempo | 2 min to ~100 BPM | 2 × 1 min to a slow beat |
| Standard | the move's dose | the move's dose | the move's dose |
| Heavy | the dose, weak side first, linked to a known move | the dose, travelling, 125 BPM and up | the dose, weak side leading, full power only while clean |
| Challenge | one full track, both sides, linked, no drops | a whole track at 140 BPM or double time | a 3 min round at tempo, freestyling the tier |

The colours are DDR's: light blue, yellow, red, green, purple.

If the 3 × 5 was meant as fifteen fixed steps instead, the content maps
straight across: every tier is ordered easiest first.

224 moves were written for these paths. The moves that existed before
(staff figure-8s, the dance steps, the first flow-prop drills) keep their
ids and are slotted in at their tier. Every move was researched against
practitioners' own material; where that material is thin, the writers
kept cues general and said so rather than invent — **buugeng**, the
**levitation wand**, **flags** and some **dragon staff** rolls are the
places a practitioner's eye is most worth having, as are **loose legs**
and the **farmer** in house. One dragon staff move was cut because no
written source confirmed it.

## Where the paths live

In **Practice**. Each prop you have, and Dance and Dance combat if you
carry them, is one card; its page has tier tabs, chart pills, and each
move's dose at the chosen chart with a dot for every chart it has been
cleared at. Logging a move at a chart counts it cleared there. Each path
remembers the tier and chart it was left on.

**Not in chimes.** A move written for a path is practised on purpose, not
pinged at a desk: chimes, "try this now", weather swaps, circuits and the
Library all leave them out. The moves that were chimed before paths
existed still are, exactly as before.

## Dance combat

There was no dance combat — only Martial basics (fundamentals) and a
staff combat drill filed first under flow, then under martial. It is its
own pack now: capoeira (ginga to meia lua de compasso), combos on the
beat, tricking (tornado and pop 360 to butterfly and 540), and staff
dance-combat, with staff combat rounds and the sword form on its path.
Solo only — nothing needs or instructs a partner. Aerials need grass, a
mat or sand, and each has a progression and a stop cue.

**The Super Hero** is a new archetype for it: *fights the way they dance,
and dances the way they fight* — Dance combat, Dance, Flow arts, Martial,
Jumps and Runs. The Operator carries it too; a Raver or a Flow Artist
does not.

## Decided: posting content

Asked to decide on the operator's behalf.

**No posting features.** No reminders to post, no schedules, no links into
any social app. The README says this app is what its author does
*instead of* scrolling TikTok, with no account and no server; a posting
calendar would pull toward the feed it replaces and turn practice into
output owed to an audience.

**Filming stays, as practice.** *Film one round, watch it once* is in
Dance, Dance combat and Flow arts. The video is yours; the app never sees
it.

**No milestone card, for now.** A shareable image would need a native
rendering dependency for something nobody has asked for yet. If people
want to share a streak or a cleared path later, render it on the phone
and hand it to Android's share sheet — opt in, no SDKs, nothing tracked.
