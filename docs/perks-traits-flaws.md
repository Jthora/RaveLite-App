# Perks, traits and flaws — a recommendation

**Short answer: flaws yes, under a better name. Traits no. Perks only if
they never touch a number.**

The instinct is right — the app has a lot of RPG furniture and not much
*agency*. But two of the three proposals would quietly break the thing
that makes the rest of it work.

## What the app already has

- **Fifteen attributes**, 0–100, grown by use.
- **A character level**, from XP, costing 500 × n.
- **Harmony days**, grades from D− to A+, streaks, tested maxes.
- **Corrections** — UCS, APT, Hourglass — already stored on the profile
  and already steering which drills appear.

That is a lot of scoreboard. The question is not "would more be fun" but
"what is missing that more would fix".

## What is actually missing: agency

Attributes are **passive**. They go up, and you watch them go up. Nothing
on the character sheet is a decision. Every good RPG progression system
is a series of choices with costs — that is the part this app does not
have, and the part worth adding.

So the diagnosis is right. It is the prescription that needs care.

## Flaws: yes, and they already exist

`corrections: ['UCS', 'APT', 'Hourglass']` **are flaws.** Upper Crossed
Syndrome is a rounded-shoulder, forward-head posture from years at a
desk. It is a real thing, the app already knows about it, already picks
drills for it, and currently presents it as a settings checkbox.

Presented as a flaw — named, explained, with a visible condition for
clearing it — it becomes the best content in the app:

> **Upper Crossed** · Fire
> Years at a desk have shortened the front of your chest and switched off
> the muscles between your shoulder blades.
> *Clears at:* wall angels 3 × 10 without your ribs flaring, held across
> four weeks.

That is zero new mechanics. It is a rename, a card, and a condition. And
unlike a perk, it is **true** — the app is not inventing a status, it is
finally saying out loud what it has been quietly training around.

It also gives the honest version of agency: you choose which flaw to work
on, and the program leans that way.

## Perks: only as records, never as modifiers

Here is the trap. A perk that gives **+10% Focus XP** or **one extra set
before the ramp cuts** would make the character sheet a game score rather
than a record of what you did.

Everything in this app rests on one claim: **the number means what it
says.** 85% of prescribed sets is 85% of prescribed sets. A tested max is
a tested max. The moment a perk inflates one of those, the sheet stops
being a measurement and starts being a save file — and then the honest
feedback loop that makes the ramp work is gone. You would not be able to
trust the app's read on you, which is the only reason to let it choose
your training.

What *is* safe is recognition of things that really happened:

> **Two-Minute Plank** — held 120 seconds, 4 Aug
> **Hundred Days** — answered a chime on 100 separate days
> **Off-Hand** — staff figure-8 as long on the left as the right

Those are records. They change nothing, they cannot be gamed, and they
cost nothing to trust. Call them **marks**, not perks — the app already
uses "marks" for standards, and the word is honest about what they are.

## Traits: no

"Morning person", "quiet flat", "no floor space" — every trait worth
having is already a **Fact**, and Facts already drive the program. A
trait system would be a second vocabulary for the same data, and two
vocabularies for one thing is how a settings screen starts lying. This
is the same reason three packs were cut in Phase 3.

## What I would build, in order

1. **Flaws** — rename corrections, give each one a card (what it is, why
   you have it, what clears it) and a visible condition. Reuses the info
   card system entirely. *Small.*
2. **Marks** — an achievements shelf on the character sheet, derived from
   the journal rather than stored, so they cannot drift from the truth.
   *Medium.*
3. **Choosing a flaw to work on** — the one real decision, which tilts
   the focus wheel toward it for a block. *Medium, and only worth doing
   after 1.*

And explicitly not: any modifier, anywhere, ever.

## The part I might be wrong about

Video-game energy is not only mechanics — it is *texture*: colour, sound,
a glyph for everything, a level-up that feels like one. You have asked
separately for a much bigger palette and a lot more symbology, and I
suspect that gets you most of what "perks" was reaching for, at none of
the cost to honesty.

If after that it still feels flat, the missing thing is agency, and the
answer is choosing a flaw — not buying a bonus.
